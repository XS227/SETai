What about your personal day?

The calculator's personal day also uses your birth day and birth month. Algebraically it is the full-date root shifted around the 1–9 cycle by (birth day + birth month) modulo 9. Changing the birthday relabels the same calendar cycle; it does not add independent information about a lottery draw. Birth year and name are not used in this particular daily-number formula.

A full seven-number root is likewise determined by the main and Euro sum roots: reduce(main root + Euro root). Those three views are related, so a coincidence across them cannot count as three independent discoveries.

Did we find evidence of a connection?

No robust association was found after correcting the 27 comparisons together. The tests covered overall root frequencies, full-date/day/month/year relationships, and exact equal-root matches. This is an exploratory analysis of an archive already examined in earlier studies.

For fairness, the baseline counts every possible five-of-50 main-number sum exactly and every two-number Euro sum under the historical pools of 8, 10 and 12. Overall frequency tests use 50,000 null simulations per sum group. Date-association tests use 9,999 permutations within the historical pool regimes. Equal-root counts use a Poisson-binomial test. Holm correction applies to all 27 comparisons.

The most frequent root is not necessarily more likely in the next draw. Any proposed prediction rule needs to be fixed before testing it on new results. No new number-selection strategy is adopted from these rankings.
