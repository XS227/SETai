Calculation and limits

Three inputs per number: last-50 frequency, long-run frequency shrunk with a 30-draw prior, and absence scaled to the pool. Standardised features are clipped, then expanded into products and powers. A linear-programming solver chooses coefficients to maximise the strict score margin between known winners and non-winners. Degree 2, 3 and 4 were tried in that order, stopping at the first exact fit; ties use ascending number order.

Using the original fixed 20 October coefficients gives 39 main and 18 Euro-number matches over all 50 cases. Excluding the case used to fit the model, the mean is 1.020 individual-number matches per line over 49 other draws. The fair-draw expectation is 0.833. This retrospective diagnostic was built after outcomes were known and is not an independent validation or a demonstrated increase in jackpot probability.

Only prospective results after the coefficients and selections are registered can test the new fixed rule without this direct answer fitting.

1 · Does any method beat random?

Loading archives

Each p-value is the share of 1,000 simulated fair archives (150 for the strategy tests) that looked at least as extreme as the real one. Holm correction is applied across every test on this page, because running many tests produces false alarms by design: with 5% per test, a few small p-values are expected by chance alone. Strategy tests replay history draw by draw using only earlier draws and compare log-loss with a flat guess.

2 · Rows fewer players choose

Every row has exactly the same chance of winning. If you win, though, the jackpot is split with everyone who picked the same row, and players favour birthdays, 7, 11 and tidy patterns. These rows come from the least popular quarter of all possible rows, and are spread to overlap each other as little as possible.

The jackpot chance stays 1 in 139,838,160 for EuroJackpot, 1 in 5,379,616 for Lotto and 1 in 61,357,560 for Vikinglotto (6 of 48 plus the Viking number). Lower sharing raises the expected prize only in the case you win. Sharing factors are relative to an average random row; they depend on the popularity model below and are not guaranteed.

How the popularity model was checked

Winner counts in each draw’s lower prize tiers depend only on how popular the drawn numbers were, because the draw itself is random. We regress them on the drawn numbers. A positive link means players really do favour those numbers.

Source data: Norsk Tipping (Lotto, Vikinglotto) and WestLotto (EuroJackpot, 2022 rules). “Empirical” weights were fitted on the archives and accepted only if they explained at least 5% of winner-count variance on untouched later draws. Otherwise the assumed weights are used, and labelled as such. Nothing here predicts which numbers will be drawn.
