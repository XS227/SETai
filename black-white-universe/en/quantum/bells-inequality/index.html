Bell’s Inequality & Quantum Entanglement

One source, two distant particles, and correlations no local hidden-variable model can reproduce.

Scroll to separate the pair and test the correlations ↓

1A pair from one source

An entangled pair is produced together and sent to distant measurement stations.

Each side chooses a measurement setting and records a local outcome.

Each local outcome is individually random; no controllable message is sent to the other side.

Memory image: one center point giving birth to two distant points.

2Angles and correlations

Repeat the experiment many times with different settings.

The crucial object is not a single result but the statistical correlation between both sides.

Bell showed that a broad class of local hidden-variable theories must obey a statistical bound.

Quantum mechanics — and experiments — violate that bound.

3What Bell really tells us

Bell violations rule out local hidden-variable explanations for all observed correlations.

They do not provide faster-than-light signalling.

Loophole-free experiments from 2015 onward greatly strengthened the evidence.

Sources: Bell (1964); Aspect and colleagues; Hensen et al. (2015); Nobel Prize 2022.
