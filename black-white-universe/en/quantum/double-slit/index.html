PART 11 · QUANTUM EXPERIMENTS

The Double-Slit Experiment — Observation and Creation

One particle, two paths, and a pattern that appears only when we do not ask which path.

Scroll to build the pattern ↓

1Two paths, one wave

Send photons or electrons one at a time: each detection is a single dot.

After many detections, the dots build bright and dark interference bands.

This is the memory image: two slits, but many bands.

Interference comes from quantum amplitudes; no appeal to human consciousness is required.

2When we ask which path

A path detector makes which-slit information physically available.

The interference bands disappear and the distribution approaches two main clumps.

What matters is the measurement interaction and distinguishability of the paths.

Remember: which-path information ↔ loss of interference.

3The image to keep

Left: two slits → many bands. Right: path measurement → two clumps.

One picture, one rule: “If you can tell which way, you lose the stripes.”

Interpretations differ on what this means about reality.

Foundational references: Young; single-particle interference experiments; standard quantum mechanics texts.

◦Three things to remember

The image that should stay with you

  1. 1Two slits can build a many-band interference pattern.
  2. 2Which-path information destroys that interference.
  3. 3Measurement here is a physical interaction, not merely a conscious glance.
