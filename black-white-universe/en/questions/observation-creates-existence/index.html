PART 12 · OPEN QUESTIONS · 01/05

Does Observation Create Existence?

↓
Question

A. The Question

Does the act of observing truly create reality?
If no one looks, does the universe still exist in the same way—or does it wait for awareness to give it form?

Question
Science

B. Today’s Scientific Understanding

Modern quantum experiments, such as the Double-Slit and Delayed-Choice Quantum Eraser, suggest that particles behave differently when they are observed.
When unmeasured, they exist as waves of probability—spread across space like whispers of potential.
But once measured, they “collapse” into definite states, as if the universe only decides what it is after someone looks.

This has shaken our classical understanding of objectivity.
Physicists like John Wheeler and Eugene Wigner have asked whether consciousness itself plays an active role in shaping reality.
Yet no definitive answer exists. We know the pattern changes—but not why.

Science
Rumi

C. Rumi’s Reflection

“The eye of the heart creates the world it sees.
The form you witness is the mirror of your inner vision.”

For Rumi, perception is not passive—it is divine participation.
The act of seeing is the act of creation, because the “seer” and the “seen” are not separate.
He believed the universe reflects the observer’s state of consciousness; when you change your gaze, you change the world itself.

Rumi
Black & White Answer

D. The Black & White Answer

If science describes how reality collapses, Rumi explains why.
Observation is not an external act—it is the meeting point of Light (awareness) and Darkness (potential).
Existence unfolds only when these two opposites unite.

In Black, all possibilities sleep.
In White, one possibility awakens.
And the moment of observation—the “look”—is the breath where God witnesses Himself through our eyes.

So yes, the observer does create reality—but not through sight alone.
It is created through presence.
