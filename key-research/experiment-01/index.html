Ten-target pattern screening in a 160-bit random address space: a reproducible control study

PRE-REGISTRATION / METHODSSYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATIONNOT PEER REVIEWED

Research group: SETAEI Computational Research · Study: ET02 · Status: protocol + runnable simulation; no live-agent result · Permanent DOI: not assigned · Ethics: synthetic target vectors only.

Abstract

We propose a paired-control simulation assessing whether a consensus-pattern gate constructed from ten independent 160-bit target vectors improves address-space retrieval outcomes when applied to a uniform candidate stream. A single candidate can be compared with ten distinct targets at negligible additional cost relative to cryptographic key derivation, thereby increasing the number of eligible targets but not the underlying rate of candidate generation. We specify theoretical baselines for exact matches and the maximum Hamming similarity, and assess selection bias, gate retention, and a reduced 12-bit pedagogical endpoint. The present document reports no evidence of a predictive advantage and makes no claim to cryptographic key recovery. Genuine evidence would require prospectively locked methods, independent replication and end-to-end performance measured on authorized synthetic inputs.

1. Research question and motivation

Does selecting a common bit pattern across ten fixed address targets produce more successful matches per generated candidate than an unfiltered baseline? Secondary questions concern the effect on maximum Hamming similarity, acceptance rate, relative computational cost, and conditional selection bias. Visible structure in random data is not by itself predictive information.

2. Formal model

Let A be a uniformly distributed 160-bit candidate vector. Let T₁, …, T₁₀ be ten distinct independent uniformly sampled 160-bit target vectors. Their 160-bit lengths describe address outputs, not 256-bit private key material. For each target define bit agreement Sⱼ(A) and the best-of-ten statistic M(A):

Sⱼ(A) = 160 − HammingDistance(A, Tⱼ)
M(A) = max(S₁(A), …, S₁₀(A))

Conditioned on a uniformly random target (or candidate), individual agreement scores follow Binomial(160, 1/2). The idealized independent-target reference for maximum agreement is:

P(M ≤ k) = [FBin(160, ½)(k)]¹⁰

This expression describes the idealized ensemble. Once the ten target vectors are fixed, the scores have shared-candidate dependence, and exact deviations from this reference should be evaluated with conditional Monte Carlo simulation.

2.1. Exact-match probability

For m distinct target addresses sampled from a uniform 160-bit address space, the single-candidate exact-match probability is m/2¹⁶⁰. Here m=10. The expected number of address candidates before an exact match is approximately 2¹⁶⁰/10. The candidate generation throughput is not increased simply because one address is compared to ten target addresses.

P(exact match with any target) = 10 / 2¹⁶⁰ ≈ 6.84 × 10⁻⁴⁸

This result assumes uniform independent candidate-address generation, distinct target addresses, and no exploitable flaw in the generation system. Bitwise similarity between two Ethereum addresses supplies no validated distance metric for the corresponding private keys.

4. Methods

4.1. Experimental objects

The browser implementation generates ten synthetic 160-bit targets from an explicitly supplied seed and then a distinct deterministic candidate stream using a non-cryptographic PRNG. A 12-position consensus gate is derived from the ten targets only. The candidate is accepted when it matches a chosen number of those 12 majority bits. This is a reproducible statistical toy model, not a substitute for a cryptographically secure generator.

4.2. Arms

Arm A — control: compare every candidate with every target, recording exact equality, maximum Hamming agreement and a 12-bit prefix match. Arm B — screened: apply the consensus gate, compare only accepted candidates with all ten targets, and report the retained outcomes relative to all original generated candidates. Both arms share exactly the same candidate stream, eliminating confounding from generator differences.

4.3. Diagnostic test of selection bias

If the mean best-of-ten similarity among accepted candidates is higher than the unfiltered mean, this may be the mechanical consequence of selecting candidates that share bits with the known targets. It is not evidence that future unknown candidates become predictable. Report both conditional and unconditional performance to avoid this error. The 12-bit prefix endpoint exists solely because observing any complete 160-bit exact hit would be practically impossible in a small simulation.

4.4. Timing measurements

Compare generation-only, ten-target bit comparison, pattern-gate overhead, and end-to-end throughput on the same hardware and with the same sample counts. Browser simulation timings are not a benchmark of secp256k1 or Keccak-256 computations. Any claim of speed-up must be defined in units (candidates/s or comparisons/s) and checked using repeated independent runs, median timings, variability and confidence intervals.

4.5. Reproduction

Choose seed 227, 10,000 candidates, and a threshold of 8/12. Record the generated JSON, source version and execution environment. Repeat with fresh independent seeds and 40,000 samples. The same seed in the same code version must produce the same counts and histogram. Validate theoretical binomial and maximum-order reference distributions.

5. Statistical interpretation and limitations

Absence of a statistically detectable difference is not proof that a model is impossible; a positive result on a flexible or reused dataset is likewise insufficient. Tests of bit frequency, lag dependence and histogram fit are diagnostics of statistical regularity, not demonstrations of key predictability. A p-value is not the probability the null hypothesis is true. In this study, ten random targets are drawn once per run, and their pairwise distances may create small conditional deviations from ideal independent-target assumptions.

We must not extrapolate a toy-model result to deployed Ethereum account security. Here no private keys are generated, no balance lookups are conducted, and no blockchain wallet is targeted. The client-side PRNG is not suitable for cryptographic use. No results yet qualify as peer-reviewed research findings or as a PhD degree award.

7. Future work

Prospectively register a family of pre-specified patterns and conduct a series of independent synthetic controls; characterize how target-target correlation alters best-of-ten distributions; quantify filter rejection and wall-clock costs separately; compare prefix matching to full equality; and publish complete null results. Future observational data from the existing SETAEI experiment should be attached only through verified, read-only aggregate APIs with documented provenance and immutable timestamps.
