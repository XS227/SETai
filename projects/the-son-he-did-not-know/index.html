The film

A tragedy inside another tragedy.

The core story comes from Ferdowsi's Shahnameh: Rostam and Sohrab meet as warriors before either understands that they are father and son.

For this film I wanted a second emotional layer. Ferdowsi is not only the author outside the story; he becomes the frame around it. His relationship with his own son, Mehrak, turns the epic into something personal: a film about fathers, sons, distance, pride, recognition and the moment when recognition comes too late.

The competition deadline is over. The film is out. At some point a project has to stop being a timeline full of versions and become a film that belongs to its audience.

How it was built

AI did not remove filmmaking decisions. It multiplied them.

01

Research & story

Historical references, Shahnameh beats, Ferdowsi's life and a narrative frame that could make the epic emotionally legible in four minutes.

02

Character continuity

Faces, ages, armor, Rakhsh, the armband, props and locations had to survive changes in lens, light and model generation.

03

Shot iteration

Prompts became closer to direction notes: blocking, lens language, sound, negative constraints, start frames and exact end states.

04

Edit & delivery

The generated material became a film only in the edit: rhythm, sound, transitions, trims, credits, watermarking and the final 4:04 export.

“The difficult part was never generating an image that looked cinematic. It was making the next image remember what the previous one meant.”

REALGRAM · Creative branch

This film grew out of the Shahnameh product.

Shahnameh began as a game, first tested as a Telegram bot and later brought into REALGRAM. Building the game world created a second problem: how do you make the characters, places and stories visible?

That question led from AI images to consistent character systems, then motion and Higgsfield. The film competition turned that visual R&D into a standalone product: The Son He Did Not Know.
